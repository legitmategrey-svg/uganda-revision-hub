S4 • Biology

Reproduction in flowering plants

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S4 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Identify parts of a flower involved in reproduction
  • Distinguish pollination from fertilisation
  • Explain how seeds and fruits form

Lesson notes

The stamen is the male reproductive part of a flower and includes anther and filament. The carpel or pistil is the female part and includes stigma, style and ovary.

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma. Fertilisation occurs when the male nucleus from a pollen grain fuses with the female nucleus in an ovule.

After fertilisation, the ovule develops into a seed and the ovary usually develops into a fruit. Pollination may be carried out by insects, wind or other agents.

Worked example

A bee may carry pollen from the anther of one flower to the stigma of another flower of the same species.

Scenario-based revision task

A farmer observes bees visiting flowering pumpkin plants. After several days, some flowers develop into fruits containing seeds. The farmer wants to understand how this happens.

Apply your learning

  1. Define pollination. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma of a flower of the same species.
  2. Explain how bees can help pollination. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Pollen sticks to the bee while it visits a flower and is transferred to the stigma of another flower.
  3. Distinguish pollination from fertilisation. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Pollination is pollen transfer; fertilisation is fusion of male and female gametes.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for correct definitions, the transfer mechanism, and a clear distinction between the two processes.

Practice questions

  1. Define pollination.
  2. Which flower part contains ovules?
  3. State one difference between pollination and fertilisation.
Show suggested answers
  1. Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma
  2. Ovary
  3. Pollination is pollen transfer; fertilisation is fusion of male and female nuclei.

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
