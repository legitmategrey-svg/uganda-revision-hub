S3 • Chemistry

Acids, bases and indicators

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S3 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe common properties of acids and bases
  • Explain the use of indicators
  • Interpret the pH scale

Lesson notes

Acids in water produce hydrogen ions, while alkalis are soluble bases that produce hydroxide ions in water. Laboratory chemicals must never be tasted or touched to identify them.

Indicators change colour depending on whether a solution is acidic, neutral or alkaline. Blue litmus turns red in acid; red litmus turns blue in alkali. Universal indicator gives a range of colours linked to pH.

The pH scale is commonly shown from 0 to 14: values below 7 are acidic, 7 is neutral, and values above 7 are alkaline. Stronger acids have lower pH values.

Worked example

A solution with pH 3 is acidic; a solution with pH 7 is neutral.

Scenario-based revision task

A school garden club tests soil-water samples using indicator paper. Sample A turns blue litmus red and has pH 3; sample B has pH 11. Learners are reminded not to taste any sample.

Apply your learning

  1. Classify sample A and sample B as acidic, neutral or alkaline. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: A is acidic; B is alkaline.
  2. Describe the effect of sample A on blue litmus paper. (1 mark)
    Suggested answer: Blue litmus turns red.
  3. Explain two laboratory safety precautions when handling unknown samples. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Do not taste chemicals; wear eye protection/gloves and use a dropper; wash hands and report spills.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for correct classification, indicator behaviour, and practical safety reasons. Do not award a safety mark for tasting or smelling a chemical directly.

Practice questions

  1. What colour does blue litmus turn in acid?
  2. Classify pH 11 as acidic, neutral or alkaline.
  3. Why should learners never taste laboratory chemicals?
Show suggested answers
  1. Red
  2. Alkaline
  3. They may be poisonous, corrosive or otherwise harmful.

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
