S3 • Mathematics

Linear equations in one unknown

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S3 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Recognise a linear equation
  • Solve equations using inverse operations
  • Check a solution by substitution

Lesson notes

An equation states that two expressions are equal. A linear equation in one unknown has a variable whose highest power is 1, such as 3x + 5 = 20.

Keep the equation balanced by performing the same operation on both sides. Use inverse operations: addition is undone by subtraction, and multiplication is undone by division.

After solving, substitute your answer into the original equation to check that both sides are equal.

Worked example

3x + 5 = 20. Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15. Divide by 3: x = 5. Check: 3(5)+5 = 20.

Scenario-based revision task

A taxi association charges a fixed booking fee of UGX 7,000 plus UGX 2,000 per kilometre. A passenger’s total fare is UGX 19,000. Let x be the distance travelled in kilometres, and express amounts in thousands of shillings.

Apply your learning

  1. Form an equation for the fare. (1 mark)
    Suggested answer: 2x + 7 = 19.
  2. Find the distance travelled, showing each algebraic step. (3 marks)
    Suggested answer: 2x + 7 = 19; 2x = 12; x = 6 km.
  3. Check the answer in the original fare rule. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: 2(6) + 7 = 12 + 7 = 19, so the answer is correct.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for the equation, subtracting 7 from both sides, dividing by 2, units, and checking by substitution.

Practice questions

  1. Solve 2x + 7 = 19.
  2. Solve 5y - 4 = 21.
  3. Check whether x = 4 solves 3x + 2 = 14.
Show suggested answers
  1. x = 6
  2. y = 5
  3. Yes; 3(4)+2 = 14

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.

Revision checklist
