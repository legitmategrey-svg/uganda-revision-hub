The water cycle

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download P6 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Name the main stages of the water cycle
  • Explain evaporation, condensation and precipitation
  • Describe why the water cycle is important

Lesson notes

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere, powered mainly by the Sun and gravity.

Evaporation happens when heat changes liquid water into water vapour. Condensation happens when water vapour cools and forms tiny droplets, making clouds. Precipitation is water falling from clouds as rain or other forms. Water then collects in rivers, lakes, soil and oceans.

Plants also release water vapour through transpiration. Some rain infiltrates the soil and may replenish groundwater.

Worked example

Sun heats water in a lake → water evaporates → vapour cools into clouds → rain falls → water flows back to the lake.

Everyday-life activity

At home or at school, a learner meets a situation involving the water cycle. Use the lesson idea to answer the practice questions and explain how you know your answer is correct.

Practice questions

  1. What provides most of the energy for evaporation?
  2. What is condensation?
  3. Name two places where water collects after rain.
Show suggested answers
  1. The Sun
  2. Cooling of water vapour into liquid droplets
  3. Examples: rivers, lakes, oceans, soil, reservoirs

How to present answers

  1. Write neatly and answer what the question asks.
  2. For calculations, show the number sentence and answer.
  3. For explanations, give one clear fact or reason.

Suggested marking: 1 mark for each correct fact/step unless the teacher's marking guide says otherwise.

Revision checklist

  • I can explain the main idea in my own words.
  • I can solve a new question without copying the example.
  • I can check my answer and correct mistakes.
