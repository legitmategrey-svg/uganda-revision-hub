S1 • Biology

Introduction to cells

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S1 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Define a cell
  • Name basic parts of a typical cell
  • Compare plant and animal cells

Lesson notes

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of a living organism. Some organisms consist of one cell, while plants and animals are made of many cells.

The cell membrane controls movement of substances into and out of the cell. Cytoplasm is where many chemical reactions occur. The nucleus contains genetic material and controls many cell activities.

Plant cells usually have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues, and a large permanent vacuole. Animal cells do not have a cellulose cell wall or chloroplasts.

Worked example

A leaf cell may contain chloroplasts to absorb light for photosynthesis; a cheek cell does not contain chloroplasts.

Scenario-based revision task

Learners observe onion epidermis under a microscope and compare it with cheek cells. Onion cells appear regular and have a cell wall; cheek cells do not have a cell wall.

Apply your learning

  1. Identify the basic unit of life. (1 mark)
    Suggested answer: The cell.
  2. Explain two functions of the cell membrane. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: It controls substances entering and leaving the cell; it helps maintain the cell’s internal environment.
  3. Give two structures present in a typical plant cell but absent in an animal cell. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Cell wall and chloroplasts (large permanent vacuole is also acceptable as a typical difference).
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for correct biological terms and accurate functions. Accept any two valid plant-cell structures absent from typical animal cells.

Practice questions

  1. What is a cell?
  2. State the function of the cell membrane.
  3. Give two structures found in a typical plant cell but not an animal cell.
Show suggested answers
  1. The basic structural and functional unit of life
  2. Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell
  3. Cellulose cell wall and chloroplasts (in photosynthetic cells)

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
