The human digestive system

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download P5 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Name the main organs of digestion
  • Describe the path food follows
  • Explain why digestion is important

Lesson notes

Digestion breaks large food substances into smaller soluble substances that the body can absorb and use.

Food passes through the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine before waste leaves through the anus. The liver and pancreas also help digestion by producing substances that aid the process.

Teeth chew food and saliva begins digestion of starch. The stomach churns food and uses acid and enzymes. Most digested nutrients are absorbed through the small intestine. The large intestine absorbs much of the remaining water.

Worked example

Chewing a piece of cassava breaks it into smaller pieces and mixes it with saliva, making swallowing and digestion easier.

Everyday-life activity

At home or at school, a learner meets a situation involving the human digestive system. Use the lesson idea to answer the practice questions and explain how you know your answer is correct.

Practice questions

  1. Name the tube that carries food from the mouth to the stomach.
  2. Where are most digested nutrients absorbed?
  3. Give one reason why we digest food.
Show suggested answers
  1. Oesophagus
  2. Small intestine
  3. To make nutrients small and soluble enough for absorption and use

How to present answers

  1. Write neatly and answer what the question asks.
  2. For calculations, show the number sentence and answer.
  3. For explanations, give one clear fact or reason.

Suggested marking: 1 mark for each correct fact/step unless the teacher's marking guide says otherwise.

Revision checklist

  • I can explain the main idea in my own words.
  • I can solve a new question without copying the example.
  • I can check my answer and correct mistakes.
