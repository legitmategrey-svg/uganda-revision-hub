P1 • Mathematics

Counting and number recognition

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download P1 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Count objects carefully
  • Read and write numbers from 1 to 20
  • Compare which group has more or fewer objects

Lesson notes

A number tells us how many things there are. Count one object at a time and say the number words in order: one, two, three, four, five, and so on.

When counting, touch or point to each object once. Do not skip an object or count the same object twice.

Numbers can be compared. A group with 7 bottle tops has more than a group with 4 bottle tops. Four is fewer than seven.

Worked example

There are 5 mangoes and 3 oranges. 5 is greater than 3, so there are more mangoes. We write 5 > 3.

Everyday-life activity

At home or at school, a learner meets a situation involving counting and number recognition. Use the lesson idea to answer the practice questions and explain how you know your answer is correct.

Practice questions

  1. Write the numbers that come after 6, 7 and 8.
  2. Draw 4 circles and 6 triangles. Which group has more?
  3. Fill in the missing number: 1, 2, __, 4, 5.
Show suggested answers
  1. 7, 8 and 9
  2. Triangles; 6 is more than 4
  3. 3

How to present answers

  1. Write neatly and answer what the question asks.
  2. For calculations, show the number sentence and answer.
  3. For explanations, give one clear fact or reason.

Suggested marking: 1 mark for each correct fact/step unless the teacher's marking guide says otherwise.

Revision checklist

  • I can explain the main idea in my own words.
  • I can solve a new question without copying the example.
  • I can check my answer and correct mistakes.
