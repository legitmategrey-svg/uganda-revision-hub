Algebraic expressions

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S2 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Identify variables, coefficients and constants
  • Collect like terms
  • Substitute values into expressions

Lesson notes

An algebraic expression combines numbers, variables and operations. A variable is a letter representing a number, such as x. In 5x + 3, 5 is the coefficient of x and 3 is the constant.

Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power. They can be combined: 3x + 2x = 5x, but 3x + 2y cannot be combined into one term.

To evaluate an expression, replace each variable with the given value and calculate using the correct order of operations.

Worked example

Simplify 4a + 3a - 2 = 7a - 2. If a = 2, the value is 14 - 2 = 12.

Scenario-based revision task

A school buys 6 exercise books costing x shillings each and 2 more books at the same price, then pays an additional UGX 5,000 for delivery. Let x represent the price of one book in thousands of shillings.

Apply your learning

  1. Form and simplify an expression for the cost of the 8 books. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: 6x + 2x = 8x.
  2. If x = 3, calculate the book cost and then add the delivery charge of 5 (thousand shillings). (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: 8(3) + 5 = 24 + 5 = 29 thousand shillings.
  3. Explain why 2a and 2b cannot be combined into 4ab or 4a. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: They have different variables and represent unlike terms; they can only be combined if their variable parts match.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for collecting like terms, correct substitution and arithmetic, and explaining unlike terms.

These are original practice items for learning; they are not official UNEB questions or an official marking scheme.

Practice questions

  1. Simplify 6x + 2x - 5.
  2. Evaluate 3y + 4 when y = 5.
  3. Explain why 2a and 2b are not like terms.
Show suggested answers
  1. 8x - 5
  2. 19
  3. They contain different variables.

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
