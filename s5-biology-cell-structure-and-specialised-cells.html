S5 • Biology

Cell structure and specialised cells

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S5 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe the roles of major cell structures
  • Explain cell specialisation
  • Relate structure to function in examples

Lesson notes

Cells contain structures that perform different functions. The nucleus contains DNA and helps regulate cell activities; mitochondria release usable energy through aerobic respiration; ribosomes build proteins; and the cell membrane controls exchange with the surroundings.

Cell specialisation means a cell has features adapted to a particular job. In multicellular organisms, specialised cells work together in tissues and organs.

A red blood cell has a biconcave shape and contains haemoglobin for transporting oxygen. A root hair cell has a long projection that increases surface area for absorbing water and mineral ions.

Worked example

The long root hair increases contact with soil particles, helping the plant absorb water and mineral ions efficiently.

Scenario-based revision task

A clinic examines red blood cells and a plant nursery examines root hair cells. Each cell has a structure suited to its role in the organism.

Apply your learning

  1. State the role of mitochondria. (1 mark)
    Suggested answer: They are the site of aerobic respiration and release usable energy.
  2. Explain one red blood cell adaptation and how it helps its function. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: Biconcave shape gives a large surface area for gas exchange; lack of a nucleus leaves more space for haemoglobin (either explained correctly).
  3. Explain how a root hair cell’s shape helps absorption. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: The long projection increases surface area, allowing more water and mineral ions to be absorbed from soil.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for accurate structure–function links, not just naming a structure.

These are original practice items for learning; they are not official UNEB questions or an official marking scheme.

Practice questions

  1. What is the role of mitochondria?
  2. Give one adaptation of a red blood cell for its function.
  3. How does a root hair cell's shape help it?
Show suggested answers
  1. Release energy through aerobic respiration
  2. Biconcave shape; haemoglobin; no nucleus in a mature mammalian red blood cell
  3. Its long projection increases surface area for absorption.

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
