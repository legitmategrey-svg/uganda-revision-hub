S6 • Mathematics

Differentiation from first principles and basic rules

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S6 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Interpret a derivative as a gradient or rate of change
  • Differentiate simple powers of x
  • Evaluate a derivative at a given point

Lesson notes

Differentiation measures how a function changes. Geometrically, the derivative at a point gives the gradient of the tangent to the curve there; in applications it can represent an instantaneous rate of change.

For a power function y = axⁿ, the power rule gives dy/dx = anxⁿ⁻¹. A constant differentiates to zero, and the derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives.

Always simplify carefully and remember that differentiation reduces the power by one while multiplying by the original power.

Worked example

If y = 3x² + 4x - 7, then dy/dx = 6x + 4. At x = 2, the gradient is 6(2)+4 = 16.

Scenario-based revision task

A technician models the height of a moving object by y = 5x³, where y is height in metres and x is time in a simplified model. The rate of change is found by differentiation.

Apply your learning

  1. Differentiate y = 5x³. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: dy/dx = 15x².
  2. Differentiate y = 4x² − 3x + 8. (3 marks)
    Suggested answer: dy/dx = 8x − 3; the derivative of a constant is zero.
  3. For y = x², find the gradient when x = 3. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: dy/dx = 2x; at x = 3, gradient = 6.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award marks for applying the power rule, multiplying by the original power, reducing the power by one, and evaluating correctly.
