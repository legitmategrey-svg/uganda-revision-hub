Integers and the number line

Lesson notes • worked example • practice • suggested answers

Download S1 notes PDF

Learning support material. Follow your current school syllabus and teacher’s guidance.

Learning outcomes

  • Recognise positive and negative integers
  • Compare integers using a number line
  • Add integers with the same sign

Lesson notes

Integers are whole numbers, their negatives, and zero. Examples are -4, -1, 0, 2 and 9. Fractions and decimals such as 1/2 and 2.4 are not integers.

On a number line, numbers increase as you move right and decrease as you move left. Therefore -2 is greater than -5 because -2 lies to the right of -5.

When adding integers with the same sign, add their sizes and keep the sign. For example, -3 + -2 = -5. When signs differ, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

Worked example

-6 + 2 = -4 because 6 has the larger absolute value and its sign is negative.

Scenario-based revision task

A school store records money owed and money received. A debt is represented by a negative number. One club owes UGX 3,000, receives UGX 4,000, then spends UGX 11,000 from its balance.

Apply your learning

  1. Represent the debt of UGX 3,000 as an integer. (1 mark)
    Suggested answer: -3,000
  2. Work out the balance after receiving UGX 4,000 and then spending UGX 11,000. Show the steps. (3 marks)
    Suggested answer: -3,000 + 4,000 = 1,000; 1,000 - 11,000 = -10,000. Final balance: UGX 10,000 owed.
  3. Explain what a negative final balance means in this situation. (2 marks)
    Suggested answer: The club has spent more than it had; it owes UGX 10,000.
Teacher-style marking guide

Award 1 mark for correct integer, 1 for each correct operation/step, and 1–2 for interpreting the negative balance clearly.

Practice questions

  1. Arrange -3, 4, 0 and -1 in ascending order.
  2. Calculate -5 + -7.
  3. Calculate 8 + (-11).
Show suggested answers
  1. -3, -1, 0, 4
  2. -12
  3. -3

How to present answers

  1. Read: underline the command word (state, describe, explain, calculate, compare or suggest).
  2. Use evidence: select facts or figures from the question scenario.
  3. Explain: give a clear reason or show each calculation step, including units where needed.
  4. Conclude: state the final answer or recommendation directly.

Marking guide: award credit for relevant facts, correct method, application to the scenario, and a justified conclusion. Accept other scientifically/mathematically correct answers.
